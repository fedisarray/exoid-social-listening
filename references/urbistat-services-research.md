# UrbiStat: services and product research

Research date: 2 November 2026
Scope: English public product and service pages, the uGeo feature and module descriptions, sector coverage, client list, recent publications and uGeo update posts. The site's sitemap contains 154 URLs, including translated pages and a long historical article archive; this note focuses on the current service-bearing pages and representative recent examples rather than summarizing every archived post.

## What UrbiStat does

UrbiStat is a geomarketing and retail market-research company focused on existing and proposed commercial sites. Its offer combines three things: the uGeo software, maintained business and territory datasets, and expert-led custom studies. It serves retail property investors, developers, property managers and multi-location retail chains. The company says it works across more than 35 countries and has over twenty years of experience. Coverage claims vary across its pages: the homepage says 30 countries for uGeo, the software page says more than 25 for analysis, and the company overview says more than 35 countries overall. Treat these as broad claims rather than a precise module-by-module coverage promise.

The portfolio is designed to answer commercial property questions such as: Where should we open? How large should a new mall be? Which competitors draw from the same residents? What happens to tenant sales if a rival opens or the site expands? Is the tenant mix sustainable? What do visitors think of the experience?

## uGeo: the software product

uGeo is UrbiStat's flagship web-based geomarketing platform. It runs on desktop and mobile devices, uses Google Maps for mapping and georeferencing, supports six interface languages, and is configured by subscription according to the functions, databases and countries needed. The site describes a mobile app for Android, with selected modules for registered users. It says reports can be printed or exported to Excel and PDF; some sector analyses also export to Google Earth.

| Capability group | What uGeo describes |
|---|---|
| Administrative and thematic mapping | Map and compare population, households, age, foreign residents, employment, income, consumption and businesses by administrative areas. Users can build thematic maps from their own imported data. |
| Catchment areas | Analyze residents and consumers inside drive-time or other time/distance areas, then compare those areas with municipalities, regions or other administrative units. The site stresses that a catchment area is an observed/modelled market area, while an isochrone is a travel-time boundary. |
| Competition and overlap | Explore existing competitors and planned projects, estimate competing attractions and overlapping catchments, and compare multiple candidate locations. Competitors can come from UrbiStat's databases or be added manually. |
| Scenarios and market models | Model current or future competitors, openings, closures and expansions. Editable gravity-model parameters estimate market share and prospective revenue; chain-specific statistical models, including multiple regression, estimate turnover for new stores. |
| Sector and brand | Filter and map retailers or centers by category, brand, size, performance and geography. Heat maps represent demand, competitor supply and areas with potential. |
| Customer and loyalty data | Upload customer/source data for mapping and analysis. Fidelity-card analysis covers customer locations, spending clusters, retention, switching, average receipts and market shares over time. |
| Real estate | Combine a catchment area with property values and trends, household counts, and the client's own property portfolio. |
| High streets and pedestrian activity | Classify retail potential on major shopping streets and show pedestrian-flow trends for about 1,000 street segments in Italian historic centers. |
| Mobile activity | The site says the module covers roughly 13 million Italian devices and supports estimates of visits, traffic, dwell time, repeat frequency, customer origin/destination and competitor overlap. The underlying data source and privacy method are not explained on the public page. |
| Client data and collaboration | Import Excel, KML, administrative-area data or pasted records; geocode address lists; filter and inspect records; share client databases among multiple users. The site says it provides confidentiality and periodic backups. |

### Typical software workflow

The geomarketing guide describes a repeatable analysis flow:

1. Place or select the proposed store or asset on the map.
2. Choose the map scale and catchment definition.
3. Select competitors from the database or add them manually, including future scenario players.
4. Compare territory data in maps, tables and charts.
5. Save the analysis and export or print a report for a business decision.

This makes uGeo an interactive decision-support workspace, not only a static map viewer. Users can adjust model assumptions and inspect how location, competition, territory and store performance change the result.

## Consulting and research services

UrbiStat groups its services by the customer's role.

### For investors

- **Diagnosis:** A cross-functional assessment of a shopping center using desk research and consumer research across marketing, leasing, management and design. It looks at target versus actual visitors, catchments, traffic and sales, tenant mix and productivity, customer satisfaction, layout and cold areas.
- **Acquisition:** Assess whether tenant occupancy costs are sustainable relative to sales, and where rental income may grow. UrbiStat benchmarks tenants by region, category and format, then combines this with a location/catchment study and SWOT analysis.
- **Impact:** Estimate the effect of a new competitor on a center's visits and tenant sales. The public page says UrbiStat uses a location-adjusted gravity model for traffic and a statistical model that considers factors such as size, distance and sales per visit for tenant sales.
- **Expansion:** Model added gross leasable area (GLA) against visits, tenant sales, rent and competitive position. Gravity models estimate visit changes; statistical models estimate sales per visit and tenant performance under alternative size and tenant-mix scenarios.
- **Food and beverage:** Estimate the right size and category mix for food-court space using a model based on performing food courts in over 100 EMEA shopping centers. Ad hoc consumer surveys add local preferences and brand demand.

### For developers

- **Location analysis:** A short initial assessment of site conditions, visibility, barriers and transport access; drive-time or walk-time catchments; population and retail/leisure consumption; and nearby competitors, including GLA, units, anchors, footfall and sales where available.
- **Dimensioning and performance forecast:** If project size is open, compare GLA scenarios against visits per square meter, sales per square meter and rent per square meter. If size is fixed, estimate visits and tenant sales, identify market gaps and develop a tenant/GLA program.
- **Market positioning:** Use consumer surveys to define the intended audience and the center's attributes. UrbiStat may coordinate workshops with property, development, design, leasing and marketing stakeholders to align on the positioning.

### For retail property managers

- **Consumer surveys:** A managed five-phase engagement: kickoff and objectives, agency/method selection, materials and calendar, fieldwork by a certified agency, and in-house analysis/reporting. Topics can include visitor profiles, shopping preferences, operations, competition, tenant mix, experience and campaign effectiveness.
- **Catchment analysis:** Map where visits originate, characterize the residents in the area, and analyze overlap across a portfolio to assess market share, penetration and loyalty.
- **Tenant-mix appraisal:** Benchmark brands using UrbiStat's database of average store size, sales per square meter, rent per square meter, effort rates, price points and assortment. Use the comparison to identify leasing opportunities by category or visitor profile.

## Datasets and sector specialization

UrbiStat says it maintains georeferenced lists of shopping centers, retail parks, factory outlets and development pipelines across Italy and more than 35 countries. Center records can include GLA, food anchors, store counts, leading brands, parking and annual visits. For grocery, the company describes databases of retailers, sales area and turnover; for fashion it offers competitor coverage, consumption studies, loyalty data and regression models. The site also names pharmacy, DIY, optics, gyms, cinemas, pet stores and home-hygiene retail.

The uGeo news archive is evidence that these datasets are actively maintained: 2026 update posts list country-specific demographic, income, consumption, labor and administrative-boundary updates, often with the public statistical office and reference date identified. That refresh work appears to be part of the product value, alongside the software itself.

UrbiStat's [2026 retail-center poster](https://www.urbistat.com/poster-top-shopping-centres-retail-parks-and-factory-outlet-in-italy-2026/) makes the inventory concrete: it maps shopping centres, retail parks and factory outlets across Italy, provides ranked location tables with GLA and annual visits, and labels the annual-visit data as a 2025 UrbiStat source.

## Commercial model and delivery

The software is sold through personalized subscriptions and quotations, with country, database and function scope selected for the client. The website invites prospects to request a demo and contact support by email; it does not offer a public self-serve checkout or list fixed prices. UrbiStat also offers custom-branded geomarketing software with client-provided information, selected territorial data, and user roles ranging from administrator to basic user.

Consulting and research are project-based and coordinated by UrbiStat specialists. Data collection for surveys is delegated to certified field agencies; UrbiStat defines the study, plans materials and timing, analyzes the results and reports them. This lets the company sell both reusable software access and higher-touch expert work.

## How this connects to Monitora

Monitora is a distinct UrbiStat review-analysis product. Its public materials focus on ratings, sentiment, reviewer behavior and store/brand comparisons. uGeo focuses on territory, competition, catchments, visits and retail performance. The products share the same retail-location context, but the public site does not document shared accounts, a software integration or a common API.

There is a useful signal of how the businesses complement one another: UrbiStat's 2026 shopping-center publication links to separate UrbiStat and Monitora posters. The UrbiStat poster presents the location database and annual visits; the Monitora poster analyzes online reviews for the same shopping-center and outlet categories. Monitora's ranking chart also uses annual visits. This suggests the group can combine store performance and customer reputation in its analysis, but the exact data-sharing arrangement is not public.

## Open questions before building a similar service

- Which official, licensed and proprietary data providers feed each country and module, and how are updates checked?
- What are the exact gravity-model equations, calibration data, assumptions, confidence intervals and validation results?
- How are catchment areas and customer visits distinguished, especially between modeled estimates and observed mobile-device data?
- What is the provenance, consent basis, aggregation and retention policy for the 13 million-device mobile dataset?
- Which uGeo modules can a client configure directly, and which outputs or models require UrbiStat analysts?
- How do Monitora and uGeo share point-of-interest identities, annual-visit estimates, accounts or reports?
- What are the actual client roles, access controls, collaboration flow and report templates inside the logged-in application?
- How does pricing vary by country, data package, modules, users and expert services?

The public evidence points to a defensible core beyond map software: a maintained retail POI database, country-level demographic and economic data, calibrated performance models, field research, and expert interpretation.

For a Monitora-like product, this suggests the core data model should center on a normalized retail location (POI) connected to a brand/network, sector, geography, source records and time-series measures. Review analytics can then sit alongside licensed footfall, catchment and competitor data. The last integration is a product opportunity suggested by UrbiStat's materials, not a confirmed description of its internal architecture.
